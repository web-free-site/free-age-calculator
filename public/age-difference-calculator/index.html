Whether you are checking how many years separate two siblings, curious about the gap between you and a partner, or filling in a form that asks for “age difference in years and months,” you need a method that respects how calendars actually work—not just subtracting birth years.

What “age difference” means on a calendar

The age gap between two people is the exact age of the younger person measured from the older person’s birth date to the younger person’s birth date. That sounds backward until you remember that both births happened on fixed calendar days. The span from the earlier day to the later day is unique, and it decomposes into years, months, and days the same way any other date interval does.

For example, if Person A was born on 10 January 1980 and Person B on 22 November 1985, the gap is 5 years, 10 months, and 12 days—not simply 1985 minus 1980.

How the calculator works

This page uses the same core logic as Free Age Calculator (from the open-source Age-Calculator project). Steps:

  1. Parse and validate both dates (invalid days like 31 February are rejected).
  2. Identify which birth came first.
  3. Count full years from the earlier date to the later date; if the later month/day has not yet been reached in the final year, subtract one year.
  4. Count remaining months, borrowing from the month when the day number requires it.
  5. Count leftover days after borrowing.

The tool also shows total calendar days between the two birthdays—the raw day count sometimes requested on immigration or genealogy forms.

Worked example

Earlier: 14 March 1992   Later: 02 August 1998

From March 1992 to March 1998 is six full years. From 14 March 1998 to 02 August 1998: March 14→April 14→May 14→June 14→July 14 = 4 months; then 14 July to 02 August = 19 days. The difference is 6 years, 4 months, and 19 days.

Edge cases and leap years

February and varying month lengths. When the day on the later birth is smaller than the day on the earlier birth, the algorithm borrows days from the previous month (28–31 days depending on the month). That is why manual subtraction often drifts off by a day.

29 February. Someone born on a leap day has a valid birth date. Comparisons use that calendar day; in non-leap years some institutions use 28 February or 1 March for anniversaries, but the mathematical difference between two fixed dates still uses the stored calendar date.

Twins born minutes apart on different calendar days. If one twin was born just before midnight and the other after, the calendar gap may be 0 years, 0 months, and 1 day even though the real-time gap is hours.

When to use years-only vs exact age

Headlines often round to whole years (“six years apart”). Forms that ask for years, months, and days need the decomposed format this tool prints. Always match the format the recipient expects.

For a child’s age on one specific day—such as a school intake cutoff—use the school admission age calculator instead, which pairs one birth date with an admission date rather than two births.

Frequently asked questions

How is the age difference between two people calculated?

The tool treats the earlier birth date as the starting point and the later one as the end date. It counts complete years, then months, then days—the same calendar-age method used on the main Free Age Calculator—so the gap matches “how old the younger person was when the older one was born” measured forward.

Does it matter which birth date I enter first?

No. Results are the same either way. The calculator automatically uses the earlier date as the reference and tells you which person is older.

Can I use this for siblings or couples?

Yes. Any two valid calendar birth dates work, including twins born on different days or partners many years apart.

Is this age difference calculator free?

Yes. Everything runs in your browser. Dates are not sent to a server and no account is required.
