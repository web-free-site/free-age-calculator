School brochures often say a child must be “5 years old by 1 September” or “turn six during the academic year.” Parents need the child’s exact age on the cutoff date, not a guess from the birth year alone. This page computes that age and explains what the numbers do—and do not—mean for admission.

What schools usually ask for

Most intake forms want either:

  • A yes/no check against a minimum age on a specific day; or
  • The child’s age in years, months, and days on that day for placement in reception, kindergarten, or Year 1.

Districts pick their own cutoff: common examples include 1 September, 31 December, or the first day of term. This calculator does not embed any of those by default—you type the date your paperwork specifies.

How the math works

The algorithm is standard calendar age from date of birth to cutoff date—the same as the home calculator’s school enrollment section and our enrollment guide.

Example (illustration only): birth 15 April 2020, cutoff 1 September 2026 → 6 years, 4 months, 17 days. Whether that qualifies for a given program is entirely up to the school’s published rules.

Examples around the world—verify locally

Policies differ. These are frequently cited patterns parents encounter; they are not legal advice and may be outdated:

  • Some U.S. states use September 1 in the entry year; others use different dates or allow waivers.
  • Parts of the UK talk about “compulsory school age” and term start dates rather than a single national birthday cutoff.
  • Indian CBSE or state boards may reference age on 31 March of the academic year.
  • Australian states set their own kindergarten entry birthdays.

Always read the current handbook from your school or ministry of education.

Leap-year births and prematurity

Cutoff math uses calendar dates, not gestational age. A child born on 29 February is still compared on the cutoff the same way as any other date; some schools publish extra guidance for leap-day births.

Medical prematurity adjustments (corrected age) are clinical decisions and are outside this tool.

Next steps

After calculating, contact the school office with the official birth certificate. For testing-age paperwork, see the chronological age calculator. For comparing siblings’ spacing, use the age difference calculator.

When the numbers sit near the cutoff

Parents whose children are born a few days before or after the published cutoff are often the most anxious. A child who is 4 years, 11 months, and 28 days on a “must be five” cutoff is not five in calendar terms even though the gap feels tiny. Conversely, someone who turned five six months earlier is clearly eligible on age alone—but waitlists, zoning, or language tests may still affect placement.

Document everything: screenshot or print this result with the cutoff you typed, then compare it with the school’s written policy. Policies sometimes distinguish “age on 1 September” from “age by the first day of school” when those dates differ by a week. Re-run the calculator if the district moves the intake day.

Repeating the calculation next year

If your child misses this year’s cutoff, advance the cutoff field to the following academic year in one step. The age will increase by one year minus any shift in the cutoff date itself. Families applying in multiple countries should run separate calculations because each nation’s cutoff is independent—this tool does not store or compare them for you.

Frequently asked questions

How do I use this school admission age calculator?

Enter the child’s date of birth and the cutoff date your school or district publishes. The tool prints exact years, months, and days on that cutoff.

Does this apply official government admission rules?

No. Laws and district policies vary and change. The example September 1 button is only a demonstration—always confirm the real cutoff with your school.

What if my school uses age five by 31 March?

Type that date into the cutoff fields. Any valid calendar cutoff works.

Is data stored?

Calculations stay in your browser; nothing is uploaded.
